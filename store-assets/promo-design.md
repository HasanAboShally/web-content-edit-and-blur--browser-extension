# Editorial Redaction

Editorial Redaction treats privacy as a visible act of authorship. Space is calm and deliberate, with a clear foreground and only a few decisive marks. The composition should communicate through contrast and placement before any words are read, as if every interval had been measured and refined through countless revisions.

Warm paper, near-black ink, and one vivid orange form the entire material vocabulary. Orange signals the active edit; black signals information deliberately removed. Nothing glows or floats without purpose. Every edge and color relationship should feel meticulously calibrated by someone working at the highest level of visual craft.

Scale creates the rhythm: one substantial statement, one recognizable product surface, and a small number of supporting details. Repetition is controlled and functional, borrowing from proof sheets and editorial markup rather than decorative pattern. The result should feel painstakingly assembled, never templated.

Composition stays asymmetric but balanced. Generous negative space gives the product action authority, while a cropped interface fragment supplies evidence rather than explanation. Text is sparse and integrated as form: a product name, a short imperative, and no paragraph copy.

The quiet conceptual reference is a document passing through review: selected, marked, and safely cleared for circulation. Redaction bars and selection outlines become the visual grammar. The final artifact must look deeply considered and masterfully finished, with every alignment surviving both a large marquee and a tiny store tile.
